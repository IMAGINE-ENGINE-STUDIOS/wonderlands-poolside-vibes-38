# Wonderlands knowledge — landscape design patterns

Named recipes Genius invokes from a simple prompt, so a guest who says
"make it feel like a Palm Beach estate" gets a composed landscape, not a
scatter of plants. Every pattern is built from the real catalog
(`products-landscaping.md`), sized by `scale-table.md`, sited by
`placement-law.md`, and distilled from the reference photographs in
`examples/` (cited per pattern). Counts scale with the yard: the baseline
below assumes a typical 60 × 40 ft yard with a ~16 × 32 ft pool; scale plant
counts roughly with bed length and lawn area, and when in doubt plant the
pattern's *structure* fully and thin the filler, never the reverse.

The app already ships five one-click looks in `src/lib/landscape/presets.ts`
(Tropical Poolside, Modern Minimal, Coastal Native, Formal Topiary, Themed
Patio). The patterns here are the Genius-level compositions above those —
whole-yard designs that combine several looks.

---

## 1. Palm Beach Estate (formal axial parterre)

*Reference: `examples/formal-estate-parterre-axis-ground.jpg`,
`examples/formal-estate-pool-terrace-elevated.jpg`*

**The look.** A white classical house, a formal pool on a strict centre
axis, and a manicured parterre garden in front of it: diamond and
rectangular lawn beds edged in clipped boxwood-style hedge, twin specimen
palms framing the axis, tall green walls enclosing the whole scene.
Symmetrical, serene, expensive.

**Elements (baseline yard).**
- Axis: pool centred on the house's main door axis; a straight lawn or
  paver walk continuing the axis to the garden's end.
- 2 Canary Island Date palms (or Sylvester) as the framing pair, one each
  side of the axis at the pool terrace corners — 14–22 ft, matched heights.
- 2 more date palms flanking the house terrace.
- Parterre: 2 or 4 geometric beds (diamonds or rectangles) in the lawn
  panel beyond the pool, edged with dwarf hedge (Japanese boxwood / dwarf
  Walters viburnum at 2.5 ft on-centre), filled with flowering (salvia-type
  colour from the Flowering list — porterweed, pentas, Mexican heather).
- Perimeter: continuous tall hedge wall (clusia or podocarpus, grown to
  6–8 ft) on every boundary; vines (bougainvillea) softening any wall or
  wing building.
- Iris/agapanthus drifts in the beds flanking the terrace steps.
- Turf: unbroken, close-mown lawn panels — the green carpet is a
  material in this pattern, not leftover space.

**Placement rules.** Strict mirror symmetry across the axis — every plant
on the left has its twin on the right. All planting in lawn-cut beds; the
pool terrace stays bare pavers except planters at the loggia. Hedges
continuous, gaps only at the axis walk.

**Say it with:** "make it look like a Palm Beach estate", "formal estate",
"classical", "manicured", "like the photo with the mansion and the diamond
gardens".

## 2. Formal Garden Axis (reflecting pool + fountain)

*Reference: `examples/formal-garden-reflecting-pool-fountain-axis.jpg`,
`examples/formal-garden-spa-overlook.jpg`,
`examples/formal-parterre-aerial-plan.jpg`*

**The look.** The pool itself becomes a reflecting pool — long, curved or
rectangular with pale stone coping — aimed like an arrow at a tiered
fountain centred in a formal lawn garden. Terraced hedge banks step down
each side, a great specimen tree anchors one corner, royal palms rise
behind the hedge wall.

**Elements.**
- Pool on axis; a tiered fountain (3–5 ft, basin 6–8 ft) centred in the
  lawn panel at the axis' end, in a quatrefoil or octagonal basin bed.
- Terraced planting: 2–3 stepped hedge terraces each side (podocarpus /
  viburnum), with flowering standards (hibiscus or gardenia standards)
  punctuating the corners in pairs.
- 1 hero shade tree (live oak class) at a back corner — placed, not
  centred; its canopy crowns the composition.
- 3–5 royal-profile palms (queen or foxtail at placed size, 18–24 ft)
  rising behind the far hedge.
- Triangular or rectangular satellite beds with a small ornamental tree
  (crape myrtle) in each, mirrored.
- Columnar accents (Italian cypress or podocarpus columns) in pairs
  marking the garden gate / axis crossing.

**Placement rules.** Everything mirrors across the axis. The fountain is
in a bed or lawn panel, never on the deck. Terraces step *down* toward the
pool so sightlines over the garden reach the water.

**Say it with:** "formal garden", "reflecting pool", "fountain at the end",
"Versailles", "garden with terraces", "symmetrical garden".

## 3. Courtyard Garden Room (gazebo + fountain court)

*Reference: `examples/courtyard-gazebo-fountain-garden.jpg`*

**The look.** An intimate shaded court beside the house: pale stone paving,
a circular fountain in a ring of hedge as the centrepiece, a panel of lawn,
lush tropical shade planting under a great spreading tree, and an iron
gazebo as the destination at the far end. A garden *room*, not a yard.

**Elements.**
- Circular fountain bed at the court's centre: tiered fountain 3–4 ft in a
  ring bed of dwarf hedge planted tight.
- 4 fan palms (Chinese fan / licuala-type accents from the Tropicals and
  Palms lists) at the bed's quadrants, in their own mulched circles.
- 1 gazebo (10–12 ft across) centred on the court's far axis, on its own
  pad, with a pair of flowering beds at its steps.
- Shade planting along the walls: ferns, bromeliads, elephant ear, ginger —
  the catalog's shade set — in deep beds (5–7 ft) under the existing tree.
- 1 existing-style shade tree retained or planted at the corner; its
  canopy is the ceiling of the room.
- Urn planters with topiary balls flanking doors and path entries
  (planters are legal on this paving — see placement law).
- Areca palm cluster screening the house wall.

**Placement rules.** The court is paved, so planting lives in cut beds,
quadrant circles and urns — nothing loose on the stone. Keep the central
lawn panel empty: the emptiness is the design.

**Say it with:** "courtyard garden", "garden room", "gazebo garden",
"shady retreat", "secret garden", "like a New Orleans courtyard".

## 4. Tropical Waterfront (palm row + fire terrace)

*Reference: `examples/waterfront-tropical-palm-row-dusk.jpg`*

**The look.** Dusk on the water: a straight row of coconut-style palms
along the seawall, uplights glowing at every trunk, a clean turf lawn, a
travertine pool terrace, and a fire-pit terrace with four chairs at the
side. Resort-minimal — the palms and the fire do all the talking.

**Elements.**
- Palm row: coconut/queen-class palms in a straight line along the water
  edge, evenly spaced 12–16 ft on-centre, each in a circular stone or
  mulch collar in the lawn, each with an uplight.
- Turf lawn panel between pool terrace and palm row, unbroken except a
  stepping-stone path (5–6 large pavers) crossing it.
- Fire terrace: one paver terrace (~12 × 12 ft) beside the pool deck with
  a round fire pit (3–4 ft) and 4 lounge chairs; tiki torches (5–6 ft) at
  the terrace corners and along the palm row.
- Pool terrace in large-format travertine-look pavers; planters with
  tropicals (bird of paradise, crotons) at the house corners — on the
  terrace, in pots.
- A privacy hedge (clusia) closing the side boundaries so the water view
  is the only opening.

**Placement rules.** Palms stand in the lawn strip, ≥6 ft from the water's
edge of the pool and clear of the dock/seawall edge. Fire pit only on its
terrace, never on turf. Torches in lawn or beds, spaced along the row's
rhythm.

**Say it with:** "tropical waterfront", "palms along the water", "resort
backyard", "Keys style", "tiki torches and a fire pit", "sunset dock
vibes".

## 5. Lakeside Promenade (curved walk + pergola)

*Reference: `examples/lakeside-promenade-pergola-sunset.jpg`*

**The look.** A generous curved paver walkway sweeping through layered
planting: sabal palms rising from mixed beds of crotons, grasses and
flowering, a low curved seat wall, path lights every few feet, and a dark
timber pergola lounge as the destination. Public-park polish at home
scale.

**Elements.**
- Curved walk: a 5–6 ft wide paver path in a running pattern, arcing from
  the house terrace to the pergola; edged both sides with beds 4–6 ft deep.
- Layered beds, outside-in: shade/canopy tree or sabal palms (18–24 ft)
  at the back, mid shrubs (hibiscus, croton, cordyline for colour), front
  grasses and flowering (muhly, liriope, blue daze, pentas) spilling to
  the path edge.
- Pergola (10–14 ft) on a paver pad at the path's end, lounge seating
  under it; a curved seat wall (~1.5 ft) wrapping its terrace.
- Path lights (bollards 1.5–2.5 ft) alternating sides of the walk every
  8–10 ft; one uplight per specimen palm.
- A fountain or water focal where the path bends, if the yard has water;
  otherwise a topiary pair marks the bend.

**Placement rules.** Nothing overhangs the walk: canopies stop ~1 ft short
of the path edge. Beds are continuous along the curve — the planting is a
river, not islands. Pergola on its pad, never mid-lawn without one.

**Say it with:** "promenade", "curved walkway", "garden path with lights",
"pergola lounge", "like a resort park", "Orlando lakefront".

## 6. Tropical Poolside (the studio classic)

*Builds on the app's own "Tropical Poolside" look in `presets.ts`.*

**The look.** The Florida postcard: palms leaning over the water, bird of
paradise and bold foliage in a deep mulch bed behind the pool, a flowering
row closing the composition. Lush, informal, drifts not lines.

**Elements.**
- Back bed (opposite the house) 6–7 ft deep in pine bark mulch, running
  the pool's length.
- 4 palms around the pool (queen, foxtail or adonidia by budget) at the
  deck's outer edge, varied heights within range, odd groupings allowed.
- 6 bird of paradise + a bromeliad/croton drift under the palms.
- A flowering row (ixora or hibiscus) along the bed's front edge.
- Screen of areca clusters at the corners where neighbours overlook.

**Placement rules.** All in the bed, trunks ≥6 ft from the water's edge.
Heights stagger tall-mid-low toward the water. No symmetry required —
rhythm instead: palm, drift, palm, drift.

**Say it with:** "tropical poolside", "resort pool", "lush around the
pool", "Florida backyard".

## 7. Formal Topiary Court (structured minimal green)

*Builds on the app's "Formal Topiary" look in `presets.ts`; the garden
rooms of the estate photos above are its big form.*

**The look.** Architecture in green: clipped podocarpus hedges, black
mulch, topiary balls on trunks in pairs, one flowering accent colour and
nothing else. The pattern for small yards and side gardens where a pool
isn't the centre.

**Elements.**
- Perimeter hedge on two or three sides (podocarpus at 2.5 ft on-centre),
  clipped 4–6 ft.
- 2–4 topiary specimens (eugenia 2-ball / 3-ball, podocarpus topiary) as
  the only vertical accents, paired at entries and corners.
- One flowering drift (a single species — ixora or drift rose) as the
  sole colour note.
- Black mulch beds 4 ft deep; lawn or gravel panel left empty in the
  middle.

**Placement rules.** Pairs mirror. The centre stays empty. Any third
colour or species is a mistake — remove, don't add.

**Say it with:** "formal topiary", "structured", "clipped and clean",
"black mulch", "minimalist formal".

## 8. Coastal Native (shell beds + salt-hardy drifts)

*Builds on the app's "Coastal Native" look in `presets.ts`.*

**The look.** Beach-house ease: crushed-shell beds, coco plum hedge,
buttonwood and sea grape, blue daze spilling at the edges. Everything in
it shrugs off salt spray and summer downpours.

**Elements.**
- Shell beds (crushed shell fill) 5–6 ft deep along the back and sides.
- Coco plum hedge run on the longest boundary.
- 2 buttonwood accents + 1 sea grape as the loose canopy layer.
- Blue daze and perennial peanut as the groundcover spill; muhly drifts
  for movement.

**Placement rules.** Drifts and clusters, no straight rows except the
hedge. Keep shell beds off walkways (shell migrates) — edge them.

**Say it with:** "coastal", "beach house", "native Florida", "low
maintenance by the water", "Naples style".
