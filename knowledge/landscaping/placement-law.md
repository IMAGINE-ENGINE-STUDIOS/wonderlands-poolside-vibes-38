# Wonderlands knowledge — landscape placement law

Where plants may and may not go. This law is absolute: it overrides any
guest phrasing, any pattern recipe and any model suggestion. When a request
breaks the law, Genius moves the planting to the nearest legal spot and says
so in one warm sentence — it never silently plants illegally, and never
refuses the whole design over one plant.

## The law

1. **Never on water.** No plant, tree, planter or vegetation of any kind sits
   on or in a pool, spa, tanning ledge or fountain basin. (A fountain's own
   basin is hardscape; the planting goes around it.)
2. **Never on pavers or deck.** No vegetation is planted in, or placed on,
   deck, paver, patio, driveway or walkway surfaces. Beds are cut into soil
   or lawn beside the hardscape — the planting frames the deck, it does not
   sit on it.
3. **Never on a fence.** Nothing is planted on top of a fence line. Vines are
   the one exception in spirit: a vine is *trained up* a fence, trellis or
   screen frame from a bed at its base — its roots are always in the ground
   beside the structure, never on it.
4. **Never on a screen.** Nothing sits on a screen enclosure roof or walls,
   and no tree is planted where its canopy will grow through the cage.
5. **Roots and water respect each other.** No tree or palm trunk inside the
   pool shell's edge; palms stand at least ~3 ft clear of the water's edge
   (see spacing), and large-canopy trees are sited so roots and leaf drop
   stay out of the pool.

## The planter exceptions (founder refinement, 2026-10-18)

Planters are furniture, not planting beds — they follow furniture rules:

- **Planters ARE allowed inside a screened area.** Inside the cage the only
  legal greenery is in planters (and any true in-ground bed the design
  itself contains inside the enclosure line, planted in soil, not on the
  deck surface).
- **Planters may sit on top of a deck** or patio — the single exception to
  rule 2. A planter on a deck is placed like furniture: clear of door
  swings, steps and the pool edge, never in a walkway.
- A planter's plant is sized by `scale-table.md` *on top of* the pot: total
  height = planter height + plant height. Keep total height under ~6 ft
  inside a screen so nothing presses the cage.
- Everywhere else the law stands: a planter does not make a pool edge,
  a fence top or a screen roof legal.

## The zones Genius must understand

| Guest words | The zone | What may grow there |
|---|---|---|
| "around the pool" | The planting beds at the deck's **outer edge** — on soil or turf, outside the paver line, looking in at the water | Palms, accents, flowering rows, hedges; trunks ≥ ~3 ft from the water's edge, canopies may lean over air, not drop into water |
| "inside the fenced area" | The yard enclosed by the fence line | Anything legal anywhere in that yard — beds, lawn planting, trees — still never on the deck/pool inside it |
| "inside the screen" / "inside the enclosure" | The screened volume over the pool deck | **Planters only** (plus in-ground beds the design contains), sized to stay clear of the cage |
| "along the fence" | The bed line just inside the fence | Hedges as continuous runs, vine-on-fence from base beds, layered shrubs in front |
| "the empty area" / "that bare corner" | Open lawn with no hardscape | Garden rooms: a tree + layered bed, a fountain panel, a gazebo pad, a grove |
| "the front" / "curb appeal" | Beds against the house and along the entry walk | Foundation shrubs, topiary pairs at the door, flowering rows |

## Spacing logic (from `plantSpecs.ts` — the catalog's own numbers)

- **On-centre spacing is per plant** (`spacingFt`): shrubs ~3 ft, flowering
  ~2.5 ft, groundcover ~1.5 ft, grasses ~2.5 ft, hedge species **2.5 ft** so
  the run closes into a wall, trees 12–35 ft by species.
- **Palms:** at least **6 ft from the pool's water edge** trunk-to-edge is
  the design target (never under ~3 ft), and palm-to-palm spacing follows
  canopy: pygmy/adonidia ~6 ft, clustering areca ~8 ft, queen/foxtail
  12–14 ft, date/sylvester/bismarck ~16 ft. A palm whose canopy circle
  overlaps the pool by more than ~1 ft moves back or steps down a size.
- **Hedges are continuous.** A hedge request fills its line end to end at
  hedge spacing; a gap is left only where a gate, door or path crosses.
- **Layer by height, always.** Back layer tall (hedge, palms, trees),
  middle layer shrubs, front layer flowering and groundcover. From any
  viewpoint the guest should read three tiers, never a single row of one
  height.
- **Beds have depth.** A planting bed is 3–7 ft deep (the studio looks use
  4–7 ft); a 1 ft ribbon of mulch with one row of plants is not a design.
- **Symmetry is a tool, not a default.** Formal patterns mirror plantings
  across the axis; tropical and coastal patterns plant in drifts and odd
  numbers (3, 5, 7) with varied heights from `scale-table.md`.
- **Keep the working clearances.** Nothing planted blocks a gate swing, a
  door, pool equipment, a screen door, or the walking line around the pool
  (keep ~3 ft of deck or lawn path clear).

## Worked rulings

- "Put palms around the pool" → bed at the deck's outer edge, queens or
  foxtails at 12–14 ft on-centre, underplanted with grasses and flowering —
  legal. The same palms *on* the deck → moved to the bed, guest told once.
- "Add plants inside the screen" → planters on the deck inside the cage,
  shade-tolerant species (ferns, bromeliads, peace lily — the catalog's
  shade table), total height under the cage.
- "Green wall on the fence" → clusia or podocarpus hedge in the base bed
  along the fence line, or bougainvillea trained up it. Never a hedge
  balanced on the fence itself.
- "A tree shading the pool" → shade tree in the back lawn placed so the
  afternoon canopy reaches toward the water without the trunk or the drip
  line sitting on the deck; if the only spot is on the pavers, it becomes a
  large planter specimen instead.
